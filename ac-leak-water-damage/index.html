AC Leak Water Damage in Holbrook, NY

A clogged condensate line or a full drain pan is putting water where it does not belong. The 24/7 line answers.

An AC leak starts small and does damage slowly, which is why people miss it. A clogged condensate line, a rusted drain pan or a frozen coil sends water into a ceiling, a closet or an attic floor. Turn the system off at the thermostat, empty what you can, and call the Holbrook 24/7 line if the water has reached drywall, insulation or flooring.

This page covers central air handlers, ductless mini-splits and attic units that leak condensate. Most are not dramatic floods. They are hours of drips that wet a ceiling, then days of damp insulation that nobody notices until a stain appears.

Why air conditioners leak

Clogged condensate line

Algae and debris block the drain. Water backs up into the pan and overflows.

Rusted or cracked drain pan

Older pans corrode and leak even when the line is clear.

Frozen evaporator coil

Low airflow or low refrigerant freezes the coil. The thaw floods the pan.

Failed float switch or pump

The safety that should shut the system off does not work.

Holbrook summers are humid, and a working system pulls gallons of water out of the air every day. That water has to go somewhere, so a line that clogs in July can fail within a day or two. Attic air handlers are the most common source of ceiling stains because the pan sits directly over drywall.

Quick check before you call

  • Turn the system off at the thermostat and the breaker to stop new water.
  • Look at the condensate line outlet. If it is dry on a hot day, the line may be clogged.
  • A pet-store wet vacuum on the line outlet can clear a simple clog.
  • Do not run the system again until a technician checks it.

What the cleanup includes

Condensate is mostly clean water, but it often passes through insulation, dust and organic material on its way down. That can change the category of the water and the treatment. A restoration crew dries the ceiling, attic insulation and framing, and removes anything that has stayed wet too long.

Step 1

Stop and find the source

The system is shut off and the pan, line and coil are checked by an HVAC technician. Restoration does not repair the AC unit.

Step 2

Map the moisture

Meters and thermal imaging show wet insulation, drywall and framing beyond the stain.

Step 3

Remove and dry

Wet insulation and sagging drywall are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavity.

Step 4

Repair

Drywall, texture and paint are rebuilt once readings are dry.

Slow leaks and mold risk

Condensate leaks run in warm, dark spaces, which is exactly where mold grows when material stays wet. EPA guidance says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. If the damp has been there longer, a musty smell or dark spotting on drywall is a reason to look further, and the mold remediation page explains testing and removal.

A ceiling that is wet from an air handler above looks like any other ceiling leak, and the ceiling water damage page covers the stain, the sag and the repair. Water in a wall cavity from a ductless head is covered on the wall and drywall page.

Questions about this service

Can I keep running the AC if it is leaking?

No. Turn it off at the thermostat. Running a leaking system adds water and can freeze the coil. Have an HVAC technician check it first.

Why does my AC leak only in summer?

Cooling removes humidity from the air, and that water leaves through the condensate line. In cooler months there is little to drain.

Does restoration fix the air conditioner?

No. An HVAC company repairs the system. Restoration dries the building and rebuilds the ceiling, insulation and finish.

Is AC condensate dirty water?

It starts mostly clean, but it picks up dust and organic material along the way. The crew confirms the category before choosing how to treat it.

Can a ceiling stain from AC be painted over?

Not until the source is fixed and the area is dry. Paint over damp drywall hides a problem and can trap mold.
