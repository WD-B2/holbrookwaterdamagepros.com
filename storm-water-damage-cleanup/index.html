Emergency Storm Water Damage in Holbrook, NY

Wind-driven rain, a damaged roof or a stressed foundation let storm water in. The 24/7 line starts the response.

Storm water damage is an emergency because the weather that caused it may still be coming. If water is entering through a roof, window or wall, protect people first, contain the water with buckets and plastic, and call the Holbrook 24/7 emergency line. If there are downed wires, a gas smell or structural damage, call 911 first.

This page covers water that enters a house during or after a storm: roof leaks, failed flashing, blown-off shingles, broken windows and water pushed through walls or foundations. Water that rises from the ground or crosses a yard is covered on the flood damage cleanup page.

Long Island storms and what they do to a house

Nor'easters, summer thunderstorms and tropical systems all hit Suffolk County. On August 12 and 13, 2014, the National Weather Service recorded 13.57 inches of rain at Islip, a New York State 24-hour record, with a 12.57-inch report from Holbrook. Roads closed, basements filled and roofs leaked across the area.

Wind-driven rain is the quiet culprit in many storms. It pushes water sideways under siding, around windows and past flashing that holds up in ordinary rain. Water then runs inside walls and shows up far from the opening.

Storm safety first

  • Stay away from downed power lines and call the utility and 911.
  • Do not go on a wet roof. Call a roofer for temporary protection.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only from a dry position.
  • Watch for sagging ceilings that may hold water.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and the gas utility.

When storm water in the house is an emergency

Water that is actively entering, a ceiling that is sagging or a roof that is open to the sky is an emergency, and the 24/7 line is the first call. Contain what you can, move valuables, and let the crew arrange temporary protection and drying while the storm passes.

What the cleanup includes

Step 1

Make it safe

Hazards are identified and temporary protection such as a tarp or board-up is arranged.

Step 2

Extract

Standing water is removed from floors and low areas.

Step 3

Map and open

Meters and thermal imaging find water in ceilings, insulation and wall cavities. Wet material that cannot dry is removed.

Step 4

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings meet a dry standard.

Rain that came through a roof is usually clean when it falls but picks up dust and debris on the way. Water that came through a foundation or from a drain is treated as more contaminated. The crew confirms the category on site, following ANSI/IICRC S500.

A leak that reached a ceiling is covered on the ceiling water damage page. Water in a lower level follows the basement water damage page. Storm-driven moisture in walls follows the wall and drywall page.

What to do first, and what not to do

  • Do not enter water that touches outlets, cords or a panel. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk first.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water or run fans across suspected mold.
  • Do shut off the water source, lift valuables and photograph the damage.
  • Do keep children and pets out of water that came from a drain or outdoors.

Questions about this service

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Holbrook?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, including during storms. Same-day response is offered when available and no arrival time is promised. In a major storm, expect that crews triage by need.

Can you tarp my roof?

Temporary protection can be arranged as part of the response. Permanent roof repair is a roofer's job.

Is rain through a roof covered by insurance?

Often yes if the roof was damaged by the storm. Flood damage from rising water is usually excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Why is the stain far from the roof damage?

Water travels along decking and rafters before it shows. The stain is rarely under the opening.

Should I wait until the storm ends to call?

No. Call so the plan starts. You will be told how to contain the water and stay safe in the meantime.
