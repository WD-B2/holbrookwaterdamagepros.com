Permit to Repair Water Damage in Holbrook, NY

Drying rarely needs one. Rebuild work that touches plumbing, electrical or structure often does.

24/7 emergency line
(934) 223-1875Call Now

By Holbrook Water Damage Pros · Published November 13, 2026 · General information, not legal or insurance advice.

Whether you need a permit to repair water damage depends on what the repair involves, not on the water. Drying and basic cosmetic repairs usually do not need one, while work that touches plumbing, electrical systems or structure often does. In Holbrook the answer also depends on which town covers your address, so the safest step is to ask that town's building office before work begins.

This guide gives the general pattern, not a legal answer for your address. Rules differ by town and change over time, and a contractor who tells you not to worry about permits for work that needs one is passing the risk to you. Unpermitted work can cause trouble at sale or at an insurance claim.

Jobs that usually do not need a permit

  • Water extraction, drying and dehumidification.
  • Cleaning and disinfecting surfaces.
  • Painting and routine cosmetic work.
  • Replacing drywall in an existing wall in some towns, depending on scope.
  • Replacing carpet or flooring like-for-like.

Why Holbrook has two answers

Holbrook is located mainly in the Town of Islip, and the section between Portion Road and the Long Island Rail Road tracks is in the Town of Brookhaven, according to its public record. Each town has its own building office and application process. Ask the right one by giving your full street address.

The Town of Islip's building division lists a Building Permit Application for most work, a separate Plumbing/HVAC permit requirement and electrical inspection certificates from qualified agencies. It also publishes guidance titled 'When is a Permit Required?' that you can read before you call. The Town of Brookhaven has its own process.

Questions to ask the building office

  1. Does my planned repair need a permit at this address?
  2. Do plumbing, electrical or mechanical parts need separate permits or inspections?
  3. Who may pull the permit, the owner or a licensed contractor?
  4. What inspections are required, and in what order?
  5. How long does review usually take?

Restoration crews dry the building first, and permits come into play when the rebuild begins. The water heater leak cleanup page describes a case where replacement of the heater itself may involve a plumber and a permit. A rebuild after a storm may involve the roof, as the storm water damage page explains.

Records to keep once the work is done

Keep every permit, inspection sign-off and certificate in one folder. If work required a final inspection, make sure it happened and that the town has it on record. Closing out permits is a step people forget, and an open permit can surface years later when you sell or refinance.

Keep the contractor's invoice, the moisture logs and photos with the permits. Together they show what was damaged, how it was dried and what was rebuilt. Buyers, insurers and inspectors ask for these documents.

If a contractor skipped a permit you needed, talk to the building office rather than hiding it. Many towns have ways to regularize work, and handling it early costs less than handling it at a sale.

Islip, Brookhaven and your address

Holbrook is a hamlet of about 26,500 people, and most of it lies in the Town of Islip. The strip between Portion Road and the railroad tracks falls under the Town of Brookhaven. A house on one side of that line answers to a different office than the house across it, even though both have a Holbrook address and ZIP 11741.

Nearby hamlets split the same way. Bohemia is in Islip, while Holtsville is mainly in Brookhaven. When you call, give the street address, not just the hamlet name, because the hamlet name does not tell the town office which one applies.

Standards and sources

  • ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard that restoration crews use to classify water and set drying goals.
  • Rules on insurance, permits and disclosure come from your policy, your town's building office and the State of New York, so confirm with them.

Questions homeowners ask

Do I need a permit for water extraction and drying?

Generally no, drying equipment does not involve construction. Rebuild work may. Ask your town's building office.

Do I need a permit to replace drywall?

It depends on the town and scope. Ask the building office, especially if wiring or plumbing in the wall is touched.

Which town covers my Holbrook address?

Most of Holbrook is in the Town of Islip, and the section between Portion Road and the railroad tracks is in the Town of Brookhaven. The building office can confirm from your street address.

What happens if I skip a needed permit?

It can cause problems when you sell or file an insurance claim, and the town can require corrections. Ask before work starts.

Does mold removal need a permit?

The State Department of Labor's Mold Program licenses assessors and remediators for projects over 10 contiguous square feet. Ask the town about building permits for the rebuild.

Who pulls the permit?

It can be the owner or a licensed contractor depending on the work and town rules. Ask the building office.
