Kitchen Water Damage in Holbrook, NY

Cabinets, counters and kitchen floors hold water in places you cannot see. The 24/7 line starts the plan.

Kitchen water damage hides behind cabinet doors and under appliances. A slow leak under the sink or behind the dishwasher can run for weeks before a floor softens or a smell appears. Shut off the supply to the sink or appliance, empty the cabinet and call the Holbrook 24/7 line for a moisture check.

A kitchen packs plumbing, appliances and cabinetry into a small space, so a small leak often touches many materials. Water follows pipes, runs under flooring and wicks up into cabinet bases and drywall.

Where kitchen leaks start

Sink supply and drain

Slip joints, supply lines and shutoff valves loosen over time.

Dishwasher

Door gaskets, inlet valves and drain hoses fail.

Refrigerator line

An ice maker or water dispenser line drips behind the unit.

Disposal and faucet

Cracked housings and loose connections leak into the cabinet floor.

Many Holbrook homes were built in the 1960s and 1970s, and kitchens have often been remodeled since. A remodel can put new cabinets over old plumbing, and that is where fittings fail. Look under the sink with a flashlight and run a dry paper towel along each joint.

A simple check you can do now

  • Empty the under-sink cabinet and wipe the pipes dry.
  • Run the faucet and the disposal for a minute.
  • Run a paper towel along every joint and valve.
  • Check behind the dishwasher with the door closed and the unit off.
  • Photograph any wet spot and call.

How the kitchen gets dried and rebuilt

Step 1

Stop and extract

The supply is shut off. Water is removed from floors and cabinet bases.

Step 2

Map and open

Meters check cabinet bases, drywall and subfloor. Toe-kicks and baseboards come off for airflow.

Step 3

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the kitchen. Readings are taken daily.

Step 4

Rebuild

Cabinets, drywall and flooring are repaired or replaced under a written scope.

Kitchens are one place where matching matters, because a new cabinet next to an old one shows. The scope can include repair in place where the material allows it. Appliance sources are covered on the appliance leak page, and floors that cupped are covered on the hardwood floor page.

Water that reached the ceiling below a second-floor kitchen follows the ceiling water damage page. A supply line that failed under pressure is a burst pipe.

Standards and sources

  • ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard for professional water damage restoration. It defines the three water categories and the drying goals used on this page.
  • The EPA guide A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture, and Your Home advises drying water-damaged material within 24 to 48 hours.
  • FEMA's Flood Map Service Center lets you look up whether an address sits in a mapped flood zone.

What to do first, and what not to do

  • Do not enter water that touches outlets, cords or a panel. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk first.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water or run fans across suspected mold.
  • Do shut off the water source, lift valuables and photograph the damage.
  • Do keep children and pets out of water that came from a drain or outdoors.

Questions about this service

Can wet cabinets be saved?

Solid wood cabinets often dry in place if caught quickly. Particleboard cabinets usually swell and are replaced. The crew checks the material before deciding.

How do I know if a leak is behind the dishwasher?

Look for a wet floor at the front, a softened toe-kick or a musty smell. Pull the toe-kick and look. If you are unsure, call for a moisture check.

Do you replace the cabinets?

Restoration dries and removes. Replacement and finish carpentry are part of the rebuild scope, which is written before work starts.

Will mold grow in a wet cabinet?

It can if material stays wet. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours.
