What a Water Damage Estimate Should Include in Holbrook, NY

A useful estimate is specific. Use this checklist to compare bids and spot gaps before you sign.

24/7 emergency line
(934) 223-1875Call Now

By Holbrook Water Damage Pros · Published November 13, 2026 · General information, not legal or insurance advice.

A good water damage restoration estimate names the water category, lists each affected area and material, counts equipment and days, itemizes demolition and disposal and separates rebuild from drying. A single lump sum with no detail is a warning sign. This guide lists what a useful estimate contains so you can compare bids and ask for what is missing.

Estimates in an emergency are often written quickly, and the final scope can change as hidden damage is found. That is normal. What should not change is the structure: you should be able to see what is included, what is not and how changes will be handled.

Ask for the missing items in writing before you sign.

Why separate drying from rebuild

Drying and rebuild are different jobs with different timelines. Drying happens first and ends when readings show a dry structure. Rebuild follows and can take weeks. Putting them on one line hides how much of the price is equipment and how much is carpentry, which makes comparison hard.

Equipment counts matter. Drying is priced by the equipment placed and the days it runs, so ask how many air movers and dehumidifiers are planned and how the count was chosen. The structural drying page explains how moisture readings guide that decision.

What changes during the job

Hidden damage often appears when a wall is opened or a floor is lifted. A clear estimate says how such discoveries are handled. The usual method is a written change order approved by you before extra work starts, with photos of what was found. Be wary of verbal changes.

  • Ask for the estimate in writing, with the date and the crew's contact details.
  • Ask whether the price is fixed, time and materials or insurer-approved.
  • Ask who will be on site and whether subcontractors are used.
  • Ask when drying is expected to finish and how you will be told.
  • Ask what warranty, if any, covers the rebuild work.

Do not pay a large deposit before you have the estimate and the scope. An estimate that skips the cause of loss may miss the repair that stops it from happening again. Water from a failed line may point back to the burst pipe page, while water that traveled into a wall points to the wall and drywall page.

How to compare two estimates side by side

Put the estimates in two columns and go down the checklist. Mark each section as included, excluded or unclear. Unclear is the important one, since it is where change orders come from. Call each company and ask for the unclear items in writing.

Compare equipment counts and days, not just totals. A bid with fewer machines may dry slower or leave moisture behind. A bid with many machines may be padded. Ask how the count was chosen and how readings will prove the structure is dry.

Compare rebuild assumptions. One bid may price basic materials while another assumes upgrades. Ask for the finish level in words, such as standard drywall with a knockdown texture, so you compare like with like.

Permits and inspections in the Holbrook area

Holbrook is divided mainly between the Town of Islip and the Town of Brookhaven. The Town of Islip's building division lists a separate Plumbing/HVAC permit requirement and electrical inspection certificates from qualified agencies. If your rebuild touches plumbing, heating or wiring, ask the estimate to say who pulls permits and who pays for inspections.

Owners in Holtsville, where the Town of Brookhaven covers most of the hamlet, should ask the Brookhaven building office about its own rules, and owners in Blue Point should do the same. A good estimate leaves a line for permits so there are no surprises at inspection time.

Questions homeowners ask

Should an estimate be a single number?

No. It should list areas, equipment, demolition, disposal and rebuild so you can compare bids.

What is a change order?

A written approval for extra work found after the job starts, such as hidden wet drywall. Get it in writing with photos.

Why do bids for the same house differ?

They may assume different categories, equipment counts or rebuild levels. Compare line by line.

Should I pay a deposit?

Ask for the written scope first, and be careful with large deposits. Understand what the deposit covers.

Who handles permits in Holbrook?

It depends on whether your address is in the Town of Islip or the Town of Brookhaven. Ask the building office, and ask the estimate to name who pulls permits.

Will the estimate include contents?

Only if it says so. Ask whether pack-out or cleaning of contents is included or separate.
