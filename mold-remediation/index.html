Musty smell or dark spots on the wall and not sure it is mold?

A musty odor, dark spotting on drywall or paint that keeps bubbling are the signs people search for first. They point to moisture and possibly growth, and the professional process for dealing with larger or hidden growth is called mold remediation. This page explains that process and the New York rules around it.

Remediation is different from cleaning a bathroom tile. It applies when growth covers a larger area, sits inside walls or floors, or follows a water loss. The aim is to return the space to the normal level of mold for an indoor setting, and the first step is always the water.

Mold starts with moisture

The EPA's guide to mold in homes says the key to controlling mold is controlling moisture, and that water-damaged material should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent growth. That is why mold work and water work belong together. A leak that was dried fast rarely becomes a mold job. One that sat for days often does.

Holbrook is humid in summer and sits near the Great South Bay, so basements, crawl spaces and closets on outside walls collect damp. Condensation, a slow pipe leak, a roof leak or a flood can each be the starting point.

Before you touch it

  • Do not scrub or spray large growth. It can spread through the house.
  • Turn off fans that blow across the area.
  • Wear a fitted N95 respirator, gloves and eye protection for a small cleanup.
  • Stop and call if the area is larger than about 10 square feet or the growth is inside a wall.

How remediation is done

Step 1

Find and fix the moisture

The source is stopped or repaired. Without that, growth returns.

Step 2

Contain

Plastic barriers and negative air machines with HEPA filters keep spores from spreading.

Step 3

Remove

Moldy porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet are removed and bagged. Hard surfaces are cleaned.

Step 4

Dry and clean the air

Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the space and HEPA air scrubbers clean the air.

Step 5

Verify

Clearance checks confirm the work before rebuild starts.

ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published standard for mold remediation, and it is fair to ask any company whether its crews follow it. Rebuilding comes after clearance, using the same finishes you had before.

New York State rules to know

New York's Mold Program, run by the State Department of Labor, licenses mold assessors and mold remediators for projects over 10 contiguous square feet. The law also separates the two roles, so the company that tests should not be the company that removes on the same project. Ask any contractor for its state license and check it with the Department of Labor.

Testing is a separate question. Air and surface samples can show what kind of mold is present and where, but they do not replace a visual inspection and a moisture source. Treat a test as one tool among several.

Mold remediation questions

Can I remove mold myself?

A small patch on a hard surface can be cleaned with care and protective gear. Larger areas, growth inside walls or growth after a flood should be handled by a qualified company, because disturbing it can spread it.

What do I do about black spots on my wall?

Do not scrub or spray large growth. Photograph it, find and stop the water source and keep fans off it. If it covers more than about 10 square feet or sits inside a wall, call for an inspection and read the remediation steps on this page.

Does a musty smell always mean mold?

No, but it often means moisture. Find the source with a meter and an inspection before deciding on testing.

Is mold testing required?

Not always. A visual inspection and a moisture check often tell most of the story. New York separates testing from removal, so ask about both.

Does bleach kill mold on drywall?

Bleach does not solve the moisture problem and does not reach into porous material. Moldy drywall is generally removed.

Who handles the water problem that caused it?

A plumber or contractor repairs the source. Restoration dries and removes. The line can explain the sequence.
