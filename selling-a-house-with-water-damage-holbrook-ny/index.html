Selling a House With Water Damage in Holbrook, NY

Past water damage does not end a sale. Hiding it can. Here is how disclosure works in New York.

By Holbrook Water Damage Pros · Published November 13, 2026 · General information, not legal or insurance advice.

In New York, a home seller must either give the buyer a Property Condition Disclosure Statement or give the buyer a credit at closing, and the statement asks about water issues such as leaks and flooding. Past water damage does not stop a sale, but incomplete answers can create problems later. This guide covers the basics, the paperwork that helps and when to talk to a real estate attorney.

This is general information, not legal advice. Disclosure law has details that depend on the facts of each sale, so talk to a real estate attorney in New York before you answer the form. What follows is the usual pattern and the practical steps that make a sale smoother.

The Property Condition Disclosure Statement

New York's Real Property Law section 462 requires sellers of one-to-four family homes to provide a Property Condition Disclosure Statement to the buyer before the buyer signs a contract. A seller who chooses not to complete it can give the buyer a $500 credit at closing instead. The form asks about the property's condition, including water, flooding, drainage and mold.

Answer from your own knowledge, honestly. If the answer is unknown, the form lets you say so. Where something was repaired, say what was done and keep the records. A buyer who sees a documented repair is usually more comfortable than one who finds an undocumented patch during inspection.

What buyers and inspectors look for

  • Stains on ceilings and walls, and fresh paint in odd places.
  • Musty smells in basements and closets.
  • Warped or cupped flooring.
  • Signs of past flooding or a sump pump in the basement.
  • Mold, which buyers often raise first.

Fresh paint over a stain is a common red flag, and it can look worse than an old stain with a repair record. If damage is current, fix the source and dry the area before listing. A buyer's inspector will find active moisture, and a moisture meter does not care about paint.

Fix first, or sell as is

Some sellers repair before listing. Others sell as is, with a lower price or a credit. Either is possible. Repairs with records tend to help. If you sell as is, disclose what you know, because the form still applies. The structural drying page explains the readings that show a structure was dried, which is the proof buyers like to see.

If past damage involved mold, the licensed process on the mold remediation page and a clearance report are the paperwork buyers will ask about. Ask your attorney how to describe it on the form.

Talking to buyers and inspectors

Be ready for questions and answer calmly with documents. A buyer who sees an invoice, moisture logs and photos is more likely to treat a past leak as a solved problem. A buyer who hears vague answers may ask for a larger credit or walk away.

Expect the inspector to use a moisture meter and possibly thermal imaging in suspect areas. If the readings are dry, that is a strong point for you. If they are not, you will want to know before the buyer does, which is another reason to check before listing.

Ask your real estate agent and attorney how to handle repair requests after inspection. Some sellers offer credits instead of repairs. Whatever you decide, keep it in writing and keep the paperwork with your closing records.

Selling in the Holbrook area

Holbrook is divided mainly between the Town of Islip and the Town of Brookhaven, and that matters at sale because permit and certificate-of-occupancy records live with the town. A buyer's attorney may ask for them, so find out which town office holds yours. Records for work done on a house near Holtsville may sit in a different office than records for a house near Bohemia.

Water history in the area is not rare. The National Weather Service recorded 13.57 inches of rain at Islip on August 12 and 13, 2014, with a 12.57-inch report from Holbrook, and buyers in the region know to ask about basements. A basement with a dry record and a documented sump pump is a selling point.

Standards and sources

  • ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard that restoration crews use to classify water and set drying goals.
  • Rules on insurance, permits and disclosure come from your policy, your town's building office and the State of New York, so confirm with them.

Questions homeowners ask

Do I have to disclose past water damage in New York?

The Property Condition Disclosure Statement asks about water issues, and sellers of one-to-four family homes must provide it or give a $500 credit. Talk to a real estate attorney about your answers.

What is the $500 credit?

A seller who does not complete the Property Condition Disclosure Statement gives the buyer a $500 credit at closing under Real Property Law section 462.

Should I repair damage before listing?

Often it helps, especially with records. Fix the source, dry the area and keep the invoices and moisture logs.

Will fresh paint over a stain hurt a sale?

It can raise suspicion. A documented repair is usually better than a hidden one.

Do buyers ask about mold?

Often. If mold was remediated, keep the assessor protocol and clearance report.

Who holds my permit records in Holbrook?

The building office of the Town of Islip or the Town of Brookhaven, depending on the address.
