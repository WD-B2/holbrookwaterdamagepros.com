Standing water on the floor is an emergency, and the Holbrook emergency water extraction line is open 24/7 for exactly that call. While you wait, stop the source if you can, shut off the main water valve, lift valuables above the wet line, and keep children and pets out of the water. If the water is touching outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

Water extraction is the first physical step of any wet-building job. Every hour that water sits on a floor it moves into baseboards, wall bottoms, cabinet kicks and subfloor seams. Removing the bulk of it quickly decides how much material can be dried in place instead of torn out.

When standing water is an emergency

Not every puddle needs a crew. A cup of water from a dripping fitting can be mopped and the spot dried with a fan. Call for extraction when water covers a whole room, when it is deeper than a baseboard, when it has soaked carpet and pad, or when you cannot find where it is coming from.

Before anything else

  • Do not wade through water that touches outlets, power strips or a basement panel.
  • Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing on a dry surface.
  • Call 911 or the utility for any gas smell or electrical risk before you call the restoration line.
  • Treat water that came up from a drain or from outdoors as contaminated and keep people out of it.

Why Holbrook basements and first floors flood fast

Holbrook sits on flat South Shore terrain between Sunrise Highway to the south and Portion Road to the north, with Long Island MacArthur Airport along the western edge. Heavy rain has nowhere dramatic to run, so it pools in low yards and finds its way to window wells and slab edges.

The National Weather Service event summary for the August 12 and 13, 2014 storm lists 13.57 inches of rain at Islip, a New York State 24-hour record, and a 12.57-inch report from Holbrook.

That storm is the clearest local reminder that extraction work in this area can involve whole levels of a house, not just a room. For your own address, check the FEMA flood map and ask the Town of Islip Building Division or the Town of Brookhaven about local requirements, since a strip of Holbrook between Portion Road and the railroad tracks falls under Brookhaven.

How extraction is done

The crew starts by identifying the water category, because that decides what protective gear is worn and what happens to the water and the materials it touched. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard that defines the three categories, and it is fair to ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Water category and what it means for extraction
CategoryTypical sourceWhat changes in the job
Category 1A broken supply line or an overflowing sinkExtract and dry. Porous materials can often be saved if the water is removed quickly.
Category 2A dishwasher or washing machine discharge, or water that sat for a whileExtract, clean and treat. Carpet pad and some porous materials are usually removed.
Category 3Sewage, river or surface flooding, or long-standing waterProtective gear, containment and disposal. Many porous materials are removed and nothing is guessed.

Categories follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard. The crew confirms the category on site.

Equipment depends on depth and surface. Submersible pumps handle several inches of standing water, truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull water out of carpet and pad, and wet vacuums finish edges and corners. Moisture meters and thermal imaging then show where water traveled beyond what you can see.

What happens after the water is out

Hour 0

Stop the source

Find the shutoff, close it and photograph the water before anything moves. Photos help any later insurance conversation.

Same day when available

Extract the bulk

Pumps and extraction units remove standing water and wet vacuums finish carpet, pad and edges.

Days 1 to 4

Map and dry

Moisture meters mark the wet zone. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest, and readings are taken until the structure reaches dry standards.

After drying

Decide on repairs

Wet drywall, baseboards or flooring that did not dry are removed and replaced. Each item is explained before work starts.

Extraction alone is not drying. EPA guidance says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so drying equipment is usually set the same visit. The structural drying and dehumidification process covers how that works and how long it usually takes.

Where the water came from changes the next step

Extraction treats the water that is already inside. The repair depends on the source. A split supply line is covered on the burst pipe page, and water that came in from outside is handled the way the flood damage cleanup page describes. If the water is in a finished lower level, the basement water damage page explains what happens to carpet, trim and contents.

Businesses face a different clock because downtime costs money, so the commercial water damage page covers that case. Nearby towns get the same dispatch line, including Bohemia to the west and Holtsville to the northeast.

What sets the price of extraction
  • Water categoryClean supply water costs less to handle than water that carried contamination.
  • Depth and areaA flooded room is a smaller job than a flooded floor. Square footage and how many levels are wet drive the equipment count.
  • Materials soakedCarpet and pad, hardwood, drywall and insulation each have a different save or replace outcome.
  • Drying daysMore wet material means more air movers and dehumidifiers running for more days.
  • Rebuild scopeAnything removed has to be replaced. That is quoted separately once the structure is dry.

Questions about this service

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Holbrook?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when available, and no arrival time is promised over the phone. The caller is told what to do safely while help is arranged.

How much standing water can be removed at once?

That depends on the depth and the surface. Submersible pumps move several inches of standing water and extraction units pull what remains out of carpet, pad and edges. The crew sizes the equipment after seeing the room.

Can I just use a wet vacuum from the hardware store?

For a small spill, yes. For a whole room, a household vacuum cannot pull water out of pad and subfloor, and water you cannot see keeps moving. If the area is larger than a few square feet or the water is not clean, call.

Will extraction save my carpet?

Sometimes the carpet and sometimes only the pad. Clean water caught early gives the best outcome, and contaminated or long-standing water usually means removal. The crew explains each decision before it is made.

Is the first visit priced before work starts?

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and the crew documents what it finds. No dollar figure is quoted over the phone because water depth and materials decide it.
