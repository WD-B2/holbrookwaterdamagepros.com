Bayport sits on the Great South Bay, south of Holbrook, and the 24/7 emergency water damage line answers for every ZIP 11705 address. If a storm pushes water into your house, close the main valve, lift belongings off the floor and call. Bayport crews come from the Holbrook office.

Storm season on the bay

A hamlet on the bay sees weather first. Wind-driven rain gets behind siding and around windows, and a hard nor'easter can overwhelm yard drainage. Check your own address on the FEMA flood map and ask the Town of Islip about local rules.

Wind and rain events on Long Island follow a seasonal pattern. Late summer and fall bring tropical remnants, winter brings nor'easters, and early spring thaws test roofs. The storm water damage page covers what to do in each case.

When water is spreading in Bayport

If water is climbing a wall, dripping through a ceiling or standing on a first floor, that is an emergency. Bayport has about 3,500 housing units, and about 77 percent are owner-occupied according to its Wikipedia entry, so most calls come from owners who need both fast action and clear documentation. Dispatch is available around the clock.

Streets, schools and the aerodrome

Middle Road, Snedecor Avenue and Railroad Street carry traffic through the hamlet, and Bayport Aerodrome, run by a volunteer society since 1972, is one of the few small airfields left on Long Island. Bayport-Blue Point schools serve most families. Roads and rail can slow a crew, so tell the line your cross streets.

Homes close to Brown's River and the shoreline hold moisture longer in fall. If your first floor stays damp after a storm, the structural drying page explains how measured drying works. A flooded first floor from rising outside water is covered on the flood damage cleanup page.

The first 24 hours after a Bayport storm leak

Once the water is stopped, the clock that matters is drying. A crew extracts standing water, uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water in walls and floors, then sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Readings are taken daily until the structure meets a dry standard.

Water that came through a roof usually stains ceilings, as the ceiling water damage page explains. Damp material left for more than a couple of days can grow mold, and the mold remediation page covers what happens then.

Standards and sources

  • ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard for professional water damage restoration. It defines the three water categories and the drying goals used on this page.
  • The EPA guide A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture, and Your Home advises drying water-damaged material within 24 to 48 hours.
  • FEMA's Flood Map Service Center lets you look up whether an address sits in a mapped flood zone.

Bayport water damage questions
