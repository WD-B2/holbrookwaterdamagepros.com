Water Damage in a Rental in Holbrook, NY

Tenants protect themselves and their belongings. Landlords fix the building. Clear notice and records help both sides.

24/7 emergency line
(934) 223-1875Call Now

By Holbrook Water Damage Pros · Published November 13, 2026 · General information, not legal or insurance advice.

In a rental, the landlord is generally responsible for repairing the building and its systems, and the tenant is responsible for protecting their own belongings, reporting problems promptly and keeping records. In New York, residential leases carry an implied warranty of habitability under Real Property Law section 235-b. This guide explains the usual roles, what to document and how to avoid a dispute, for tenants and landlords alike.

This is general information, not legal advice. Leases and facts differ, so check your lease and consider asking a tenants' or landlords' attorney. The pattern below helps most disputes stay small.

If you are a tenant

  1. Stop the water if you can, and keep yourself and others safe. Close a fixture valve, and call 911 for electrical or gas danger.
  2. Photograph and film the source, the water and your damaged belongings.
  3. Notify the landlord or manager right away, then follow up in writing by email or text so there is a record.
  4. Move belongings out of the wet area and keep receipts for emergency costs.
  5. Call your renters insurer if you have a policy, and ask what it covers.

Renters insurance generally covers your belongings and additional living costs when the home is unlivable. It does not cover the building, which is the landlord's property. If you do not have a policy, your claim against the landlord for belongings can be limited, so a policy is worth having before a loss.

If you are a landlord or manager

Respond quickly, even if the cause is unclear. Stop the source, call a plumber if needed and arrange drying. Prompt drying limits damage and mold, and a record of fast action protects you. The water extraction page and the structural drying page describe the first steps.

Document the unit before and after, keep invoices and moisture logs and tell your insurer. If the unit is uninhabitable, discuss temporary housing or rent abatement with your attorney. Multi-unit buildings add a twist, because water in one unit often comes from another, and the commercial water damage page covers multi-unit properties.

Common disputes and how to avoid them

  • Late notice. Report problems in writing as soon as you see them.
  • Slow repair. Follow up in writing, with dates.
  • Mold claims. Dry quickly and keep records.
  • Belongings. Tenants should insure them, landlords should not promise to.
  • Access. Give notice and agree on times for drying crews.

If mold is involved, the licensed assessor and remediator process on the mold remediation page applies, and the roles are kept separate in New York. Document before and after, and let the licensed professionals do the work.

Standards and sources

  • ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard that restoration crews use to classify water and set drying goals.
  • Rules on insurance, permits and disclosure come from your policy, your town's building office and the State of New York, so confirm with them.

Questions homeowners ask

Who pays for water damage in a rental?

The landlord is generally responsible for repairing the building, and tenants for their own belongings. Renters insurance can cover belongings. Your lease and the facts decide specifics.

What is the warranty of habitability?

In New York, Real Property Law section 235-b implies in residential leases that the unit is fit for living. Serious water problems can bear on it. Ask an attorney about your situation.

How should a tenant report water damage?

Right away, and in writing by email or text. Include photos and the date and time you found it.

Does renters insurance cover the building?

No. It covers your belongings and additional living costs, not the structure.

Can a landlord enter to dry the unit?

Landlords generally need to give notice and agree to reasonable times, except in an emergency. Check your lease and local rules.

Who handles mold in a rental?

Fix the water source and dry quickly. Larger mold work requires licensed assessors and remediators in New York.
